Liquid Effluent — Sewer and STP Workers

Annual effective dose from activity excreted by nuclear-medicine patients, adapted from NUREG/CR-5814 (1992): Scenario 1 — sewer system inspector in the interceptor (external irradiation from wastewater — water slab, receptor 1 m above its surface — plus aerosol inhalation); Scenario 2 — sewage-treatment-plant (STP / EDAR) operator of sludge dewatering equipment (external exposure to dewatered sludge + inhalation of resuspended dry sludge; retained fraction as entered (default: 100%), annual discharge / annual sludge-production screening model with transit decay only — IAEA SRS-19 (2001) §4.9).
Screening estimate, not a complete discharge assessment. Within each scenario, doses are computed per nuclide and summed linearly. The two scenarios concern different receptors and are never added together. The IS-28 Annex II II.A.4 sewer conditions (per-nuclide concentration level, sum of fractions, annual activity limits) and mixture clearance are evaluated separately. Inhalation uses dose coefficients for adult members of the public (neither worker is an exposed worker of the licensee).

Aadm = patients × cycles × administered activity per cycle; all of it is assumed to be excreted. The fraction excreted in hospital leaves through the facility's sewer connection — or, for a nuclide controlled in decay tanks, only its share of the annual tank discharge (shared among the controlled nuclides in proportion to the activity excreted into the tanks; no decay credit when their sum is below it). The rest is excreted at home. Scenario 1 receives only the activity leaving the hospital. Scenario 2 receives the activity leaving the hospital plus the activity excreted at home by the patients living in the STP catchment.

          📖 Method

          For each nuclide, with A the activity reaching the sewer in one year and DF = exp(−λ·ttransit):

          Scenario 1 (NUREG/CR-5814 §5.2.1, transit ≈ 0.2 h, dilution in the facility's annual water volume V):

          • Cwater [Bq/m³] = A · DF / V; Cair [Bq/m³] = (Cwater / ρwater) · aerosol loading · fresp
          • Eext = Cwater · k1 · text · g; Einh = Cair · BR · tinh · e(inh)

          Scenario 2 (NUREG/CR-5814 §5.2.2, transit ≈ 3 d, the year's discharge retained in the year's sludge):

          • Cdry [Bq/kg] = A · fsludge · DF / (Mwet · fsolids); Cwet [Bq/m³] = A · fsludge · DF · ρwet / Mwet
          • Cair [Bq/m³] = Cdry · dust loading · fresp
          • Eext = Cwet · k2 · text · fgeom; Einh = Cair · BR · tinh · e(inh)

          No decay during the exposure year is applied. The concentration is the annual discharge divided by the annual water volume (Scenario 1) or by the annual sludge production (Scenario 2), with transit decay only; this is a screening assumption — a continuous discharge does not by itself demonstrate mixing or steady state. fsludge is the retained fraction as entered (default: 100%). k1 and k2 are effective-dose-rate coefficients (ICRP Publication 116 (2010), ROT) calculated with PHITS for the geometry of each scenario — they are not point-source Γ constants and only apply to that geometry.

          Algorithms and defaults are adapted from the reference workbooks IRA-xxxx_Lu-177_dosis_en_escenario2_v1.xlsx (Scenario 2) and Efluentes - Dosis precisas escenarios 1_2_4 v2.xlsx (Scenario 1). Inhalation coefficients are those for adult members of the public (RD 783/2001 Annex III table b), kept by the CSN Resolution of 3 April 2024; transcription: ICRP Publication 119 (2012), Table G.1), lung absorption type F (fast) or M (moderate). Half-lives are taken from the app database (Lu-177m, not a curated entry, from ICRP 107).
